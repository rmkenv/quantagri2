## 🛰 Remote Sensing  

ESA’s **MetOp‑SG‑B1** weather satellite has just arrived at the French Guiana spaceport, a move that will tighten the cadence of European weather forecasts and give farmers a sharper picture of tomorrow’s conditions. The new satellite, slated for launch aboard an Ariane 6 later this year, will provide high‑resolution atmospheric data that can feed into precision‑ag platforms. Meanwhile, Copernicus Sentinel‑3’s latest image of West Antarctica reminds us that even the most remote ice sheets are now monitored in near‑real time, a trend that extends to the Mediterranean where ESA’s image of Toulouse’s terracotta rooftops showcases the level of detail achievable with modern optical sensors. In the commercial sector, a MarketsandMarkets report on India’s small‑satellite market signals a surge in locally built constellations that could soon deliver tailored crop‑health and irrigation data to Indian farmers. Finally, Sentinel‑3C’s capture of Hurricane Rachel over the eastern Pacific demonstrates the continued relevance of space‑borne sea‑surface temperature radiometers for tracking tropical cyclones that threaten coastal agriculture.

## 🌾 Agriculture & Crop Monitoring  

Across the ag‑tech landscape, a pause in construction at Liberation Bioindustries’ 600,000‑liter biorefinery in Richmond, Indiana, underscores the funding gap that can stall even the most promising bio‑fuel ventures. In a different vein, Danone’s experiment of turning yogurt by‑products into renewable energy illustrates how food waste can be monetised, a model that could inspire circular‑economy pilots on farms. The acquisition interest in Believer Meats’ cultivated‑meat facility highlights the growing appetite for cell‑based protein, but also the legal and financial complexities that accompany such high‑tech assets. Meanwhile, a wave of ransomware attacks across the agrifood sector has prompted a wake‑up call from industry bodies, reminding operators that cyber‑security is as critical as soil‑health. Finally, Earth Rover’s CLAWS robot, which uses pulsed light to target individual weeds without chemicals, offers a glimpse of the next generation of precision weed control that could reduce herbicide use and lower input costs.

## 💧 Freshwater & Groundwater  

Arizona’s decision to intervene in Nevada’s lawsuit against the federal Colorado River plan signals a broader regional push to protect water allocations that could be cut by the Trump‑era 10‑year strategy. In the Great Lakes, a township vote to build Michigan’s second hyperscale data centre reflects a growing recognition that data infrastructure can support water‑management decisions, though the environmental trade‑offs remain debated. At the same time, Sturgeon Fest’s release of 1,500 fish into Lake Erie is a low‑cost attempt to bolster a keystone species, while Michigan DNR’s use of 23andMe‑style gene testing to track fish stocking demonstrates how genomics can inform restoration outcomes. Across the state, communities are pouring millions into river projects, but the return on investment will hinge on whether these initiatives translate into measurable water‑quality and flood‑control benefits.
