## 🛰 Remote Sensing  

While NASA’s [Psyche mission](https://science.nasa.gov/photojournal/nasas-psyche-mission-captures-mars-during-gravity-assist-approach/) captured a colorized view of Mars, the same high‑resolution optical sensors that power its cameras are already being adapted for Earth monitoring. ESA’s [Sentinel‑1 radar image of Greenland](https://www.esa.int/ESA_Multimedia/Images/2026/05/Earth_from_Space_Greenland_s_changing_ice) demonstrates how SAR (synthetic‑aperture radar) can track glacier melt even through cloud cover, offering a template for monitoring water‑source glaciers that feed the Great Lakes. NASA’s [post‑tsunami landscape of Tracy Arm](https://science.nasa.gov/earth/earth-observatory/tracy-arms-post-tsunami-landscape/) shows how rapid satellite imaging can reveal landscape change after extreme events, a capability that can inform post‑disaster recovery planning. Together, these stories illustrate that the same space‑borne sensors that explore other worlds are now delivering actionable data for Earth‑bound stakeholders.

## 🌿 Invasive Species  

The University of Hawai‘i’s latest funding push—nearly 100 projects totalling $33 million—signals a statewide commitment to curb invasive species that threaten crop yields and ecosystem services. The initiative will deploy remote‑sensing tools to map and monitor invasive spread, giving farmers and regulators a data‑driven way to target eradication efforts. Stakeholders in the Pacific Northwest and beyond should keep an eye on how these tools are rolled out, as they could become a model for other regions.
