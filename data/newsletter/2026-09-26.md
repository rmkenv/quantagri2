The most pronounced divergence between price and NDVI velocity occurs in the **soybean** sector. In contrast, **corn** shows positive velocities in both Iowa (**+0.26527 / 16 days**, z‑score **–3.078**) and Illinois (**+0.2447 / 16 days**, z‑score **–2.97**), yet the front‑month price has slipped **‑2.62 %** over the week. The velocity z‑scores suggest a drought‑driven collapse, but the market’s weekly decline may be an over‑reaction to short‑term weather concerns.  

**Sugar** presents a rare alignment: São Paulo’s velocity is **+0.23626 / 16 days** (z‑score **+8.88**) and the price is near its 52‑week high at **$18.54**. The velocity is decelerating but remains positive, and the market is correctly pricing in the robust growth.  

**Cotton** shows a velocity of **+0.209 / 16 days** (z‑score **+4.96**) but a negative LSWI, indicating moisture stress. The price has risen **+3.31 %** over the week, suggesting the market may be over‑pricing the crop relative to the underlying moisture conditions.  

The core thesis for this week is that **soybean prices are over‑valued relative to the satellite‑derived growth trend**, while **corn and cotton prices may be under‑valued relative to their positive NDVI velocities**. Traders who can navigate these divergences may capture mispricing opportunities.
