## Spectral Alpha This Week  
The most striking divergence between satellite‑derived vegetation health and market pricing occurs in the cotton market. The front‑month cotton contract (CT=F) trades at **$76.78**, a **+2.09 %** weekly gain, placing it at **87.5 % of its 52‑week high**. In contrast, the Xinjiang cotton NDVI velocity is a modest **+0.009/16d**, barely above zero. This small positive velocity indicates that the crop is only slowly greening, and the z‑score (not provided in the table but inferred from the velocity) would likely be near zero, suggesting no significant momentum. The satellite signal therefore signals a weak or stagnant crop, while the market continues to price in a robust cotton season.

Other commodities show tighter alignment. Soybeans exhibit a strong positive velocity (+0.23465/16d in Iowa, +0.24482/16d in Mato Grosso) and a price near the 52‑week high, consistent with the market’s bullish stance. The velocity z‑scores (–7.08, –7.59) warn of a sharp decline relative to the baseline, but the price remains near the top of the range, indicating that the market may be over‑pricing the crop if the trend reverses.

Corn’s velocity is mixed: Iowa corn shows a healthy +0.22227/16d, while Illinois corn is essentially flat (–0.0006/16d). The price sits at 88.2 % of the 52‑week high, suggesting that the market is still optimistic but may be under‑pricing the crop relative to the strong Iowa signal.

Wheat’s velocity (+0.25452/16d in Kansas) is positive, yet the price is down 1.46 % for the week. The divergence is modest; the market may be reacting to short‑term supply concerns rather than a fundamental shift in crop health.

Sugar’s velocity (+0.22781/16d in São Paulo) is robust, and the price is near the upper end of the 52‑week range, confirming that the market is correctly pricing the strong sugarcane growth.

The key takeaway is that cotton’s satellite signal is the most discordant with market pricing, suggesting a potential mispricing that could present an arbitrage opportunity for traders who incorporate NDVI velocity into their models.
