### The Macro Picture

June’s NDVI aggregate reports paint a complex climate backdrop. The **soybean** velocity z‑score of **–7.265** signals severely reduced greening relative to the 2000‑2015 baseline, a red flag that the crop was under stress. Yet the **sugar** velocity z‑score of **+8.8** indicates a rapid green‑up, and the LSWI confirms above‑average moisture. **Corn** sits at a velocity z‑score of **–2.57** and a LSWI of –0.5, pointing to heat‑induced senescence but only a slight moisture deficit. **Wheat** is in a drought‑collapse quadrant, velocity –1.72, LSWI –4.01. **Cotton** shows a velocity z‑score of **+4.315** but a negative LSWI, so the crop is green‑ing but water‑stressed.

These signals dovetail with the broader weather narrative: La Nina tightening in the South, El Nino drought stress in the West, and a neutral to slightly dry regime in the Midwest. The WASDE snapshot for June shows a 2.3 % decline in U.S. soybean crop acreage, but the satellite data suggests that the remaining acres are still green‑ing. The market, however, had priced in a modest contraction, reflected in the 92‑95 % of 52‑week high range for soybeans and corn. By July, the market had adjusted, pushing prices toward the upper end of their ranges.
