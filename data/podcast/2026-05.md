**The Macro Picture**

May’s NDVI signals sit against a backdrop of a neutral to slightly drying climate. The monthly aggregate data show soybeans in a quadrant III regime (heat senescence) with a strongly negative velocity z‑score. Corn is also in quadrant III, with a negative velocity z‑score of –1.83, while wheat is in quadrant IV, a drought‑collapse scenario with a negative velocity z‑score of –1.84. Sugar, by contrast, is in quadrant I, a positive velocity and positive LSWI, signaling robust growth. Cotton is in quadrant II, a stress greenup regime: strong greening but significant moisture deficit.

These regimes dovetail with the WASDE 2026 outlook, which projected high U.S. corn and soybean production but warned of potential yield penalties in Brazil and Mexico due to late‑season drought stress. The market’s near‑peak pricing for soybeans and corn reflects the optimism around U.S. yields, but the satellite data suggest that the crop has already begun to plateau or even decline. Wheat’s price, at 91.8 % of its high, is consistent with the moderate price support that has been offered in the market, but the negative velocity points to a potential supply shortfall as the crop matures.
